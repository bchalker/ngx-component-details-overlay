Component Details Overlay

Switch it on and every tagged component on the page shows its name as it is written in code. Use it to check a live page against a design file, and to find elements built outside your component library.

This banner is hand-built and untagged, so it gets no badge. That is how an audit finds elements built outside your component set.

Each tagged component gets a dashed outline and a badge with its name. Nested components get different colours, so you can see what sits inside what. The overlay draws on its own layer and ignores the mouse, so clicks, scrolling and layout behave as they did before.

Hold Alt (Option on Mac) and click any component to copy its name for a bug report, a ticket or an AI prompt. Badge colours adjust to the page behind them so they stay readable.

Every piece of the screen has a name in the code. When you use that name in Slack, a ticket or a review, the developer knows exactly what you mean. No screenshot, no guessing, no back and forth.

It also helps product owners, QA testers and other non-technical teammates communicate clearly with developers, and gives them a small look at how the screen is built.

  1. Switch on Component details in the bottom-right corner.
  2. Find the badge on the thing you mean. The badge is its name.
  3. Alt-click it (Option-click on Mac) to copy the name, then paste it into your message. If it sits inside another component, add that first, like Card › Button.

“The pill in that table looks off on mobile.”

“Table › StatusBadge is cut off at 320px on /customers.”

No badge on something? It is untagged, so say that: “the banner on /customers is untagged.” A developer will know to look at it.
